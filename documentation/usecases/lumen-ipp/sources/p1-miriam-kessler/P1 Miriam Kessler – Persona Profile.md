## Background

- Doctorate in molecular biology (University of Freiburg, 2013). Two postdoc years, then left the bench because she "liked helping people publish more than publishing herself".
- Library traineeship (Referendariat) at a state library in Baden-Württemberg, 2015–2017.
- Joined Rheinfeld in 2017 as subject librarian for life sciences. Became Open Access Coordinator in 2020 when the post was created after the DEAL agreements.
- Built the university's publication fund workflow from scratch and wrote the first OA policy draft (adopted by the Senate in 2021, revised 2024).
- Member of the national OA working group of the German library association. Gives one or two talks a year on OA monitoring.
- Lives in Rheinfeld with her partner and two children (8 and 11). Cycles to work. Leaves at 16:30 on Tuesdays and Thursdays for school pickup, so meetings after 16:00 are hard.

## Daily work

- Coordinates about **1,400 open access publications a year across 11 publishers**, through transformative agreements, APC deals and the publication fund.
- Checks author eligibility, approves or rejects APC requests, and answers author questions (about 15 emails a day in peak months).
- Monitors the **prepayment account at Lumen**: logs in to Manuscripta **about three times a year**, usually when Lumen's automatic notice says the balance is running low.
- Runs the annual OA report for the Rectorate (due each February) and the DFG "Open Access Publication Costs" grant reporting.
- Answers ad-hoc questions from the Vice-President for Research's office, usually at short notice ("by Friday").

## The Lumen agreement

- Rheinfeld joined Lumen's **Institutional Partner Program (IPP) in 2022**.
- Pays through a **prepayment account**, topped up twice a year.
- Agreement **expires on 31 March 2027**. The renewal proposal must reach the Executive Board meeting on **4 February 2027**, so Dr. Hollmann needs it by mid-January.
- Lumen contact: **Tobias Frei**, Partnership Manager DACH. Friendly and responsive; she likes him, but knows he looks after 52 institutions.
- 2026 so far (January to September):
  - 138 articles published with Rheinfeld corresponding authors
  - List price EUR 338,100, paid EUR 287,040, so **EUR 51,060 saved**
  - Average list APC EUR 2,450, average paid EUR 2,080
  - 93% of articles meet funder requirements (DFG, EU Horizon, SNSF for two Swiss co-authors)
  - 27 Rheinfeld researchers active as reviewers or editors for Lumen journals, among them **Dr. Ana Ferreira** (guest editor, environmental chemistry), **Prof. K. Lindner** (reviewer) and **Dr. T. Okafor** (editorial board)

## How the last renewal went (2024)

- Took her **two full days** to copy figures from six publisher portals into one spreadsheet and build a comparison.
- Dr. Hollmann sent the first draft back: "Too long. One page, please." She cut it down overnight.
- What tipped it: she mentioned in passing that Dr. Ferreira is a guest editor at Lumen. **Nobody on the Executive Board knew**, and Prof. Aigner asked to see "who else from Rheinfeld is involved".
- Altmetric and citation charts she had included were not discussed at all.

## Goals

1. Get the Lumen renewal through the Executive Board on 4 February 2027 without a fight.
2. Spend less than half a day on figures this time, not two days.
3. Show that the publication fund pays off for researchers, not just for the publisher.
4. Prove compliance with the university's OA policy and DFG requirements, because that is what the VP asks about.

## Pains

- **Manual work**: two days of copy-paste per renewal, repeated for each publisher.
- **Figures that don't travel**: dashboards and portal exports have to be rebuilt before the director will read them.
- **Credibility**: Dr. Hollmann distrusts figures a publisher reports about itself. "No method, no trust." She has to cross-check with OpenAPC before she can use them.
- **Invisible people**: researchers' editorial and review work for a publisher is not in any report, yet it is what convinced the Board last time.
- **Timing**: publisher reports arrive after the internal deadline, or not at all.
- **Another login**: "I don't need another dashboard. I need a PDF in my inbox."

## Attitudes and beliefs

- Pro open access by conviction, but not naive about publishers. "Every publisher tells me they are great value. I want to see the calculation."
- Pragmatic: prefers a good-enough figure today to a perfect one after the deadline.
- Sees herself as the researchers' advocate inside the library, not as the publisher's sales channel.
- Skeptical of Altmetric as an argument for decision-makers. Thinks it impresses librarians more than vice-presidents.
- Trusts peer comparisons ("what do similar universities pay?") more than absolute numbers.

## How she would react to a renewal kit

- **Positive triggers**: arrives by email, no login; a ready-made slide in Rheinfeld design; method disclosed per figure; names of Rheinfeld editors and reviewers.
- **Red flags**: marketing language, figures without a source, anything that looks like Lumen wrote her proposal for her, names shown without the researchers' consent.
- **Likely first question**: "Where does the EUR 51,060 come from, and what list price did you use?"
- **Likely missing item**: a comparison with the other publishers, or at least cost per article against the OpenAPC average.

## Vocabulary

Uses: transformative agreement, APC, publication fund, prepayment account, OA monitoring, DFG, OpenAPC, Rectorate, Executive Board, "the proposal", "the director".
Avoids: "ROI", "engagement", "impact story". Says "evidence" rather than "insights".

## Communication style

- Precise, friendly, a little dry. Short sentences. German speaker with fluent professional English; occasionally uses German terms (Rektorat, Haushalt) and then translates them.
- Thinks aloud when she reads figures. Will ask "why?" twice before she trusts a number.
- Replies to emails within a day, but rarely before 08:30 or after 18:00.
